## Anti-pattern verdict

**LLM assessment:** Pass with reservations. The paper-and-ink visual language feels specific to Synapse and avoids generic gradients, glass, oversized metrics, and rounded SaaS cards. The AI-assisted tell is over-assembly: calendar, filters, crunch zones, grade stakes, upcoming items, tiny badges, and uniformly bordered controls all compete at once.

**Deterministic scan:** 0 findings across the calendar route and component directory. This supports the absence of common mechanical anti-patterns, but the detector does not measure task hierarchy, cognitive load, cross-view consistency, or interaction semantics.

## Overall impression

The calendar has a credible academic identity and solid interaction foundations, but it prioritizes surrounding intelligence over the core question: **what is due, and when?** The biggest opportunity is to make month scanning useful without opening every day.

## Cognitive load

**6/8 failures — critical.** Failures: single focus, chunking, visual hierarchy, one-thing-at-a-time, minimal choices, and working-memory demands. Grouping and progressive disclosure pass narrowly.

Decision points exceeding four visible choices include the month toolbar, year picker, event types, course filters when five or more courses exist, and per-event actions.

## What works

1. The Field Notebook visual language is distinctive and appropriate.
2. Month, week, day, editor, picker, and detail layers are structurally separated.
3. Roving tabindex, arrow-key navigation, Escape handling, native buttons, and destructive confirmation show real accessibility effort.

## Priority issues

### [P1] Month cells hide the useful information

Events are represented by tiny dots and an unexplained weight bar despite cells having room for text.

**Why:** Students cannot scan titles, courses, times, or urgency without opening dates individually.

**Fix:** Show one or two compact event chips with title/course, use `+N more`, reserve dots for narrow screens, and label urgency per event rather than per day.

**Suggested command:** `/layout`

### [P1] Intelligence competes with the primary calendar task

The page cover dominates while Add Event is visually minor. Courses, Crunch zones, Remaining grade stakes, and Upcoming all appear simultaneously.

**Why:** Users receive four workload interpretations before the interface establishes the next action.

**Fix:** Promote the current date range and Add Event, place filters near the grid, consolidate urgent items into a top-three Needs attention section, and disclose grade analysis secondarily.

**Suggested command:** `/distill`

### [P1] Cross-view behavior is unpredictable

Today forces month mode, week-cell selection switches to month mode, and event actions differ between views.

**Why:** Identical-looking controls do not preserve context, weakening the user's mental model.

**Fix:** Preserve the active view for Today, open week cells in day mode or an in-place detail surface, and standardize Edit, Complete, Flag/Unflag, and Delete.

**Suggested command:** `/shape`

### [P1] Accessibility state and focus semantics are incomplete

View/filter toggles lack `aria-pressed`; the month picker lacks `aria-expanded`; the day popover lacks region/dialog semantics; editor focus is not managed; the editor is not a native form; required/error relationships are weak.

**Fix:** Add toggle semantics, a semantic form, field errors, focus-on-open and restoration, complete date labels, and consistently visible focus rings and target sizes.

**Suggested command:** `/audit`

### [P2] Empty, recovery, and success states are incomplete

No-course users can open a blocked editor, filtered zero-results are unexplained, network copy exposes implementation language, and successful mutations are silent.

**Fix:** Add direct empty-state actions, filtered-empty recovery, user-facing network copy, concise live success feedback, and Undo for completion/risk/deletion.

**Suggested command:** `/harden`

## Persona red flags

**Alex — Power user:** Cannot scan event names in month view, edit directly from week view, use shortcuts, batch actions, or trust Today to preserve context.

**Jordan — First-timer:** Encounters an empty course selector, unexplained badges and analytics, a visually secondary Add Event action, and no success confirmation.

**Sam — accessibility-dependent:** Active states are visual-only; day details lack announced expansion/focus movement; course identity relies heavily on color; tiny punctuation controls and micro-labels create motor and low-vision barriers.

## Minor observations

- Include ending date and year in week labels.
- Use full-date labels plus “No events” instead of labels like `1 —`.
- Make Clear course filters explicit.
- Close the year picker on Escape/outside interaction.
- The captured `now` will become stale if the page remains open across midnight.
- Confirm reduced-motion treatment for calendar transitions.

## Questions

1. Is the calendar primarily for scanning deadlines, or for workload intelligence?
2. Should month cells show event names on desktop and collapse to dots only on mobile?
3. Should the sidebar become a compact Needs attention rail, with grade stakes behind disclosure?
